ATMChat — Privacy Policy & Terms of Use

Effective date: April 13, 2026  |  Last updated: October 14, 2026

This document contains both the Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use for ATMChat. Please read both sections carefully before using the App.

Part I — Privacy Policy

1. Introduction

This Privacy Policy explains how ATMChat ("the App"), developed and published by Approve The Move ("we", "us", or "our"), handles information when you ("you" or "the user") download, install, or use the App on any supported device.

We are committed to protecting your privacy. This policy applies to all versions of ATMChat distributed through the Apple App Store and any future platforms.

By downloading, installing, or using ATMChat, you acknowledge that you have read, understood, and agree to the practices described in this policy. If you do not agree, please do not use the App and remove it from your device.

2. Information We Collect

We do not collect your personal data: the App never sends it to us, and we operate no servers. The App does send some data to nearby devices (Section 4) and, for link previews, to the linked website (below). We receive personal data only when someone chooses to email us: your own support email or report, or a report another user sends about you, which can include your display name and device ID and, for a reported message, its text (see "Emails you choose to send" below). ATMChat is designed from the ground up as a privacy-first, offline mesh messaging application. Specifically:

  • No account creation — You are never required or asked to sign up, log in, create an account, or provide an email address, phone number, or any other personal identifier. Your device identity is a randomly generated UUID that is not derived from your Apple ID, phone number, or other personal information.
  • No analytics or tracking — We do not use any analytics frameworks, crash reporting services, telemetry tools, device fingerprinting, or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No advertising — The App contains no advertisements, no advertising SDKs, no ad identifiers (IDFA), and no advertising networks.
  • No server-side communication — We operate no servers, and the App never sends your data to us on its own. All messaging traffic is strictly local peer-to-peer. The only internet requests the App can make are link previews (page title and image) for web links you or a peer share, including links posted in the "Everyone Nearby" room. When such a link appears on screen, Apple's LinkPresentation framework fetches the preview from your device, directly from the linked website (and any server that website uses for its preview image), so that website sees your IP address. In version 1.1 and earlier, previews load automatically whenever an internet connection is available. Since version 1.2 they are off by default and load only if you turn on Settings → Privacy → Load Link Previews. These requests are not routed through us, and nothing about them is logged or collected by us.
  • Emails you choose to send — Report (long-press a message you received → Report, or a chat's options menu → Report…) prepares an email to support@approvethemove.com in Apple's Mail composer, or copies it to your clipboard if Mail isn't set up. Settings → Contact & Legal → Email Support opens your email app. Nothing is sent unless you send it. A report contains the name and ID of the chat you report from (for a one-to-one chat, the peer's display name and device ID) and, if you report a message, its type, text and time. We receive whatever you send, along with your email address, and use it only to reply to you, to review and act on reports, and to cooperate with lawful requests from authorities (Section 16).
  • No cloud sync — The App has no cloud sync, no server-side message storage, and no synchronization service of any kind, and it never sends your messages or other App data to us. Your messages are stored on your device and on the devices of the people you exchange them with (for "Everyone Nearby" posts, every device that receives them). Like any app's data, they are included in your own device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) if you use them. The device ID kept in the Keychain is not restored to a different device (Section 3.1).
  • No in-app purchases — No financial, billing, or payment information is collected or processed.
  • No location data — The App does not request or use your geographic location. Videos and files you send keep any location metadata they already contain (Section 4.6).

We do not sell, rent, or trade any personal information. Apart from emails people choose to send us, including reports, we never possess it: your data lives on your device and on the devices of the people you message, not with us.

3. On-Device Storage

ATMChat stores data locally on your device: your profile, settings and small lists in UserDefaults, your device ID normally in the Keychain (Section 3.1), and your chats, media and related files in the App's own folder (Section 3.2). This data stays within the App's private sandbox, is not accessible to other apps, and is never sent to us by the App. It leaves your device only when the App sends it to nearby devices, some of it automatically (Section 4); when a link preview loads (Section 2); when you choose to share, save, forward or report something yourself; and as part of your own device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) if you use them.

3.1 UserDefaults & Keychain

A small amount of data is stored using Apple's UserDefaults and Keychain systems:

  • Your display name and, if you set one, your profile photo (both shared with nearby devices as described in Section 4.2), and the network name other devices see during discovery, which is based on your display name
  • A randomly generated unique identifier (UUID) that identifies your device to other ATMChat devices — not derived from your Apple ID or other personal information. It is normally kept in the device Keychain, which is not restored to a different device. If the Keychain can't be used, it is kept in UserDefaults instead (as the earliest versions did), and that copy is included in device backups.
  • Display names of other ATMChat users your device has learned, either by connecting to them or from the neighbour announcements of devices it connects to (Section 4.2), not only people you have chatted with. Since version 1.2, at most 2,000 are kept.
  • Per-conversation unread counts
  • Your blocked-peers list: each blocked peer's device ID and last-known name (kept only on your device; never shared with anyone, including the blocked peer)
  • Your notification preferences (which alert types you receive), per-chat mute list, and each chat's Disappearing Messages setting
  • Since version 1.2: your Load Link Previews setting, and a count of message deliveries plus the date and app version of the last App Store rating prompt, used only to limit how often that prompt can appear

This data is declared in our Privacy Manifest (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) under API category NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults with reason code CA92.1 (access to store and retrieve user preferences), as required by Apple.

3.2 ChatStorage (Custom Local Storage)

ATMChat uses a custom on-device storage system called ChatStorage, plus a few related files in the App's own folder, to persist your messaging data locally. This includes:

  • Message history for each conversation, including the "Everyone Nearby" room: message text, sender name and device ID, time, replies, reactions (with the device ID of each person who reacted) and media details such as file names. Each conversation keeps up to 10,000 messages; older ones are removed. A deleted or expired message leaves a placeholder (sender, time and type, but no content).
  • Media files: copies of the photos, videos, voice messages, contact cards and files you send and receive
  • Conversation list (the list of peers you have communicated with)
  • Delivery status of each message you send (sent, delivered or read). The App does not record when a message was read.
  • Offline queue: text messages and links you send while the other person is out of range, kept until their device confirms delivery (up to 500 per person; beyond that, the oldest is dropped). A queued message more than 72 hours old is discarded instead of sent.
  • Profile photos that other users' devices send when they connect to yours. Since version 1.2 they are re-encoded at a small size and at most 500 are kept.

All of this data resides within the App's iOS sandbox on your device. The App never uploads or syncs it, and never sends it to us or to any server. Like any app's data, it is included in your own device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) if you use them, and is restored with them. Apart from those backups and anything you choose to share, save, forward or report yourself, message content leaves your device only when it is sent to a peer over the local network, directly or through other ATMChat devices (see Section 4), and, when a link preview loads, when the link itself is requested from the website it points to (Section 2). Since version 1.2, if the history or offline-queue file is damaged and can't be decoded, it is kept in the same folder under a new name rather than overwritten.

How to delete: You can delete a message you sent in a one-to-one chat (long-press → Delete for Everyone). Its content is removed from your device at once, and a request to delete it is sent to the other person if they are reachable. Their copy may remain: nothing is sent while they are offline, and a recipient using version 1.2 or later ignores delete requests relayed through other devices (since version 1.2, the App tells you when you aren't directly connected). In version 1.1 and earlier, a message deleted or expired while it was still waiting in the offline queue could still be sent when that person reconnected. You can also turn on Disappearing Messages for a one-to-one chat (the chat's options menu → Disappearing Messages → 30 seconds, 5 minutes, 1 hour or 24 hours). Messages older than that, sent or received, are then removed from your device when you open the App or the chat, but not from the other person's device. Apart from Disappearing Messages, the App has no way to delete messages you received, posts in the "Everyone Nearby" room (yours included), or a whole conversation. To remove all of the App's stored data, delete ATMChat from your device — iOS removes everything in the App's sandbox (message history, media, conversation list, and preferences) on uninstall. Two narrow exceptions, both standard iOS behavior: the random device UUID stored in the iOS Keychain (Section 3.1) may persist on the device after the App is deleted until it is removed, and device backups you made before deletion may retain copies of previously stored data. Neither of these ever reaches us.

4. Local Networking & Mesh Messaging

Local networking is the primary feature of ATMChat. The App enables peer-to-peer mesh messaging between nearby devices using two Apple frameworks: MultipeerConnectivity (Wi-Fi) and CoreBluetooth (Bluetooth Low Energy). This section provides comprehensive detail about how your data is handled during messaging sessions.

4.1 How It Works

  • Messaging is fully peer-to-peer — no internet connection is used or required to send or receive messages. All messaging travels over local Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), directly between devices or relayed by other nearby ATMChat devices (Section 4.3). No message data passes through our servers, Apple's servers, or any internet service. The App's only possible internet access is the link-preview fetch described in Section 2, which is automatic in version 1.1 and earlier and, since version 1.2, off unless you turn it on.
  • The App uses Apple's Bonjour protocol (via MultipeerConnectivity) and BLE advertising (via CoreBluetooth) to discover nearby devices that are running ATMChat. While the App is open, your device advertises itself, and over Bluetooth it keeps doing so while the App is in the background. The MultipeerConnectivity advertisement carries your network name, which is based on your display name, so nearby devices looking for ATMChat can see it before any connection. The App's own Bluetooth advertisement carries only the name "ATMChat", not your name or device ID (Section 4.2 describes what a device that connects can obtain). Your device connects automatically to the ATMChat devices it finds.
  • Once peers discover each other, a direct connection is established. All subsequent data exchange occurs over this local peer-to-peer link.
  • No message logs exist on any server. Because messaging never touches the internet and we operate no backend infrastructure, there is no server that could store, log, intercept, or retain any of your messages. We have no ability to read, access, or recover your messages.

4.2 Data Transmitted During Sessions

During an active local messaging session, the following types of data may be transmitted between connected devices:

  • Text messages — The content of messages you type and send to other peers.
  • Photos — Images you select from your photo library or capture with your camera to send to peers.
  • Videos — Video files you select from your photo library to send to peers.
  • Voice messages — Audio recordings you create within the App to send to peers.
  • Contact cards — Contact information you explicitly choose to share with peers.
  • Files — Documents and files you explicitly select to send to peers.
  • Delivery receipts — Confirmation signals indicating that a message has been delivered to the recipient's device.
  • Read receipts — Signals indicating that a recipient has opened and viewed your message.
  • Message reactions — Emoji reactions you attach to specific messages.
  • Delete requests — When you use Delete for Everyone on a message you sent in a one-to-one chat, a request to remove it is sent to the person you sent it to, if they are reachable. A recipient using version 1.2 or later removes a message only at the request of the person who wrote it, and only when the request arrives over a direct connection; requests relayed through other devices are ignored, so their copy may remain (Section 3.2). Delete for Everyone isn't available in the "Everyone Nearby" room.
  • Display name, profile photo and device UUID — These are sent automatically, as soon as a connection is made, to every ATMChat device yours connects to (not only people you chat with), so peers can identify each other. Your display name and UUID are also part of every message you send, including messages relayed through the mesh. Over Bluetooth, your display name and UUID are sent to any device that connects to the App's Bluetooth messaging service and subscribes to it, without pairing, so a nearby Bluetooth device that isn't running ATMChat can obtain them too. In version 1.1 and earlier, any device that connected to either of the App's two Bluetooth services could also read your UUID directly from it. The UUID is randomly generated and not derived from your Apple ID, phone number or other personal information, but it is sent together with your display name, does not change when you change your name, and can survive reinstalling the App (Section 3.2).
  • Neighbour announcements — To route mesh messages, whenever your device's connections change it tells each device it is connected to which devices it is connected to (their UUIDs), with their display names, so devices two hops away can show names. Since version 1.2 these announcements name only your current neighbours. In version 1.1 and earlier they also carried the names and UUIDs of every device yours had connected to since the App last started.
  • Typing indicators — While you type in a one-to-one chat, a signal that you are typing is sent to the person you are chatting with.
  • "Everyone Nearby" room messages — Text messages and links you send to the shared room are delivered to every ATMChat device they can reach, directly or through intermediate ATMChat devices (see Sections 4.3 and 4.4), and each of those devices shows and stores them with your display name and UUID. Anyone on the local mesh can read what you post in the room, and the App has no way to delete a room post afterwards, from your device or anyone else's — treat it like speaking up in a shared space.

All of this data is transmitted over local Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, between nearby devices. None of it is routed through the internet or stored on any server. It can pass through other ATMChat devices that relay it (Section 4.3), and because it is not encrypted in transit (Section 4.4), someone nearby with the right equipment could capture it.

4.3 Mesh Routing & Privacy Implications

ATMChat supports mesh networking, which means messages can hop through intermediate devices to reach their intended recipient. This enables communication even when two devices are not directly within range of each other, as long as intermediate ATMChat devices form a connected path.

Important privacy details about mesh routing:

  • Intermediate devices act as relays only. When a message hops through an intermediate device, the ATMChat app on that device forwards it onward automatically, including while the App is in the background. Relayed messages addressed to someone else are never displayed to the relay device's user and are never saved into that device's chat history. Posts in the "Everyone Nearby" room are different: every device they reach, relays included, shows and stores them (Section 4.2).
  • No persistent relay logs. Relaying happens in memory; intermediate devices save no copy of the messages they forward. To avoid forwarding the same message twice, a relay remembers each packet's random ID (not its content) in memory only, for at most the last 5,000 packets. A relay can also see which device UUIDs a packet is from and to.
  • Honest limitation: mesh traffic is not end-to-end encrypted (see Section 4.4). While the ATMChat app never shows relayed content, a technically sophisticated user with modified software on the relay path could in principle inspect relayed data. Treat mesh conversations like conversations in a shared physical space.

4.4 Transport Security

Local peer-to-peer sessions currently run without transport-layer encryption. This is a deliberate engineering trade-off: encrypted MultipeerConnectivity sessions proved significantly slower and less reliable over mixed Wi-Fi + Bluetooth mesh links, and dependable delivery is the App's core purpose. What this means in practice:

  • Your messages are never sent over the internet, so they cannot be intercepted remotely — an eavesdropper would have to be physically nearby, within local Wi-Fi/Bluetooth range, while you chat. (When a link preview loads, the shared link itself is requested from the website it points to; see Section 2.)
  • Within that local radio range, transmitted data is not encrypted. Treat ATMChat conversations like conversations in a shared physical space.
  • We recommend not sending highly sensitive information (passwords, ID numbers, financial details) over any local mesh network, including ATMChat.
  • A future update may add transport encryption; if the trade-offs change, this policy will be updated accordingly (see Section 31).

4.5 Session Lifecycle

When a messaging session ends (by disconnecting, moving out of range, or quitting the App from the app switcher), the peer-to-peer connection is terminated. While the App is only in the background, it keeps using Bluetooth: it can stay connected to nearby devices, receive messages and relay other people's messages, and iOS can relaunch it in the background for Bluetooth events. Message history that has been saved to ChatStorage (Section 3.2) persists locally on your device. No session data is transmitted to any server, and no session metadata is retained beyond what is stored locally on your own device.

4.6 Media & File Privacy

Photos, videos, voice messages, contact cards, and files you send through ATMChat are transmitted to the recipient's device over the local peer-to-peer connection. They are never uploaded to any server, cloud storage service, or content delivery network. Videos are sent only over a direct Wi-Fi connection. Other media up to 700 KB sent to someone who isn't directly connected passes through relaying devices on the way, in memory only (Section 4.3). Once transmitted, the media is stored on the sender's and recipient's devices (and in their device backups, if they use them); what the recipient does with it after that, such as saving, sharing or forwarding it, is up to them.

Photos you send from your library are downscaled and re-encoded on your device before sending, which also strips embedded metadata such as location (EXIF). Videos and files are sent as they are, without re-encoding, so they keep any metadata they contain (a video can include where it was recorded), and a file is sent with its original file name. If you choose to save received photos or videos to your own photo library, the App uses Apple's add-only photo access — it can add the items you explicitly save but can never read, browse, or access anything else in your library.

9. Data Security

Although the App sends us no personal data and we run no servers, we employ the following security measures to protect your experience within the App:

  • Local-only communications — Messaging traffic travels only over local radio links between nearby devices, directly or relayed by other nearby ATMChat devices (Section 4.3). There is no server, cloud relay, or internet path that could be breached remotely. Transport-layer encryption is not currently enabled on the local link (see Section 4.4), so physical proximity is the security boundary.
  • Sandboxed storage — On-device preferences and message history are stored within the App's iOS sandbox, which is isolated from other apps and protected by the operating system; the device identity normally lives in the iOS Keychain (Section 3.1).
  • Minimal network surface — The App operates no servers and initiates no internet connections other than link-preview fetches (Section 2). Since version 1.2 these are off unless you turn on Settings → Privacy → Load Link Previews, and only web (http/https) links are fetched; in earlier versions a preview is fetched automatically when a link message is shown in a chat. A fetch goes straight from your device to the linked website, which sees your IP address. Because we run no servers, there is no server-side store of your data that could be breached.
  • No persistent sensitive data — No passwords, tokens, credentials, or financial data are ever stored or processed.
  • Mesh relay hygiene — One-to-one messages routed through intermediate devices are forwarded in memory only. The relaying app never displays them and retains no copy after forwarding, only an in-memory record of recent message IDs so it does not relay the same message twice (see Section 4.3 for the honest limits of this). "Everyone Nearby" room posts are different: any device that receives one, including the devices that relay it, can display it and store it in its room history.

An honest note on security: no method of electronic storage or local wireless transmission is 100% secure, and we cannot and do not guarantee absolute security. Section 4.4 describes the App's transport-security model candidly. To the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, we are not liable for security incidents arising from circumstances beyond our reasonable control, including vulnerabilities in the underlying operating system, radio protocols, or your device.

17. Message Delivery Disclaimer

ATMChat is an offline, peer-to-peer mesh messaging application. Message delivery is best-effort only and is not guaranteed. Due to the inherent nature of local mesh networking:

  • Range limitations — Messages can only be delivered to devices within range of your local Wi-Fi or Bluetooth Low Energy signal, either directly or via intermediate mesh devices.
  • Mesh availability — If there is no connected path of ATMChat devices between you and the intended recipient, messages cannot be delivered. A message can pass through at most four intermediate devices. Over Bluetooth or the mesh, files larger than 700 KB cannot be sent, and videos can only be sent over a direct Wi-Fi connection.
  • Session dependency — Messages can only travel while the App is running on the sending and receiving devices (and on any device relaying them) and those devices are connected, directly or through the mesh. Using iOS Bluetooth background modes, the App can keep sending and receiving while it is in the background, but iOS decides when it runs: a device that is switched off, out of range, or on which the App has been force-quit receives nothing. There is no server to queue or store messages for later delivery.
  • Environmental factors — Physical obstacles, interference, device settings, and distance may affect connection quality and message delivery reliability.
  • Local queuing only — If you write a text message while the recipient can't be reached directly or through the mesh, the App keeps it in an on-device queue. It resends the message automatically when that peer is reachable again, until a delivery or read receipt confirms it arrived. Queued messages older than 72 hours are discarded unsent, and at most 500 messages wait per peer (the oldest are dropped first). Photos, videos, voice messages, contact cards and files are never queued: they can only be sent while the peer is reachable. Posts in the "Everyone Nearby" room are never queued either: they reach only the devices on the mesh when you post. There is no internet or server path for delivery, so a message to a peer who never comes back into range will never be delivered. Since version 1.2, a queued message that you delete for everyone, or that expires under a disappearing-message timer, is removed from the queue and not sent. In earlier versions it stays queued and is still delivered when the peer reconnects.
  • Best-effort delivery — Messages may be lost, delayed, duplicated, or fail to arrive without notice to you. If you share session access — for example by posting in the "Everyone Nearby" room or by chatting in a crowded environment — messages may also be received by unintended nearby peers.
  • Delete for Everyone is limited — It is available only for messages you sent in a one-to-one chat (not in the "Everyone Nearby" room). It removes the other person's copy only if their App receives the delete request. Nothing is sent to a peer who can't be reached when you delete, so their copy stays. If the other person uses version 1.2 or later, their App ignores deletes relayed through the mesh, so their copy is removed only if your devices are directly connected; since version 1.2, the App tells you when you are not directly connected. Earlier versions also accept relayed deletes. Copies the recipient has already saved to Photos, forwarded, shared or captured in a screenshot are not affected.
