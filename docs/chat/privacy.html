ATM Chat — Privacy Policy & Terms of Use

Effective date: April 13, 2026  |  Last updated: October 31, 2026

This document contains both the Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use for ATM Chat (formerly ATMChat). Please read both sections carefully before using the App.

Part I — Privacy Policy

1. Introduction

This Privacy Policy explains how ATM Chat ("the App", formerly called ATMChat), developed and published by Approve The Move ("we", "us", or "our"), handles information when you ("you" or "the user") download, install, or use the App on any supported device.

We are committed to protecting your privacy. This policy applies to all versions of ATM Chat distributed through the Apple App Store and any future platforms.

By downloading, installing, or using ATM Chat, you acknowledge that you have read, understood, and agree to the practices described in this policy. If you do not agree, please do not use the App and remove it from your device.

2. Information We Collect

We do not collect your personal data: the App never sends it to us, and we operate no servers. The App does send some data to nearby devices (Section 4) and, for link previews, to the linked website (below); and if you use iOS's Translate or Look Up on text in the App, iOS may send that text to Apple (below). We receive personal data only when someone chooses to email us: your own support email or report, or a report another user sends about you, which includes your device ID and the name their App shows for you and, for a reported message, its text (see "Emails you choose to send" below). ATM Chat is designed from the ground up as a privacy-first, offline messaging application for people nearby. Specifically:

  • No account creation — You are never required or asked to sign up, log in, create an account, or provide an email address, phone number, or any other personal identifier. Your device identity is a randomly generated UUID that is not derived from your Apple ID, phone number, or other personal information.
  • No analytics or tracking — We do not use any analytics frameworks, crash reporting services, telemetry tools, device fingerprinting, or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No advertising — The App contains no advertisements, no advertising SDKs, no ad identifiers (IDFA), and no advertising networks.
  • No server-side communication — We operate no servers, and the App never sends your data to us on its own. All messaging traffic is local, between nearby devices. The only internet requests the App itself makes are link previews (page title and image) for web links you or someone else shares, including links posted in the "Everyone Nearby" room. When such a link appears on screen, Apple's LinkPresentation framework fetches the preview from your device, directly from the linked website (and any server that website uses for its preview image), so that website sees your IP address. In version 1.1 and earlier, previews load automatically whenever an internet connection is available. Since version 1.2 they are off by default, load only if you turn on Settings → Privacy → Load Link Previews, and are fetched only for web (http and https) links. The App may request the same preview more than once, for example once more straight away, with longer to load, when the first request times out. These requests are not routed through us, and nothing about them is logged or collected by us. Some controls hand you over to other apps without the App sending anything itself: Settings → Visit Website (Safari), Rate ATM Chat (the App Store's review page), Join the Beta (TestFlight) and Contact & Legal → Email Support (your email app); Invite People (in Settings, on Welcome and on an empty Home), which gives the App Store link to the iOS share sheet; and, since version 1.2, Settings → Language, which opens ATM Chat's page in the iOS Settings app. What those apps do is up to Apple or the app you choose. Show QR Code, beside Invite People, draws the App Store link as a QR code on your device, with no internet.
  • Select Text, Translate and Look Up (iOS's own tools) — Since version 1.2, the long-press menu of a text or link message offers Select Text and Translate (Translate isn't offered on a message that is only a web address). They use iOS's own tools, not the App's. Select Text shows the message's text so you can select part of it, and iOS shows its own menu. Translate works on your device when both languages have been downloaded; otherwise iOS may send the text you translate, which can be a message someone else sent you, to Apple, under Apple's Translation & Privacy notice, which says what Apple keeps. Downloading a language also uses the internet. Look Up sends limited information to Apple, including what you look up, under Apple's Search & Privacy notice. Other items in that menu are iOS features too, and pass the text to the service or app you choose. iOS offers the same tools on text you type, and may offer them in a file you open in its Quick Look preview. On iOS 18 and later, Settings → Download Languages for Translation (in the Language section) asks iOS to download the languages ATM Chat suggests (your device's language and Arabic, or English on a device set to Arabic; Arabic and English if Translate doesn't support your device's language) so that Translate can work offline; iOS asks you first and downloads them from Apple over the internet. The App itself sends no message text for any of these tools and gets nothing back: iOS shows the result in its own view.
  • Emails you choose to send — Report prepares an email to support@approvethemove.com in Apple's Mail composer. You can report a message (long-press it → Report), a person (a one-to-one chat's options menu or Chat Info → Report…) or the "Everyone Nearby" room (its options menu). If Mail isn't set up, a sheet shows our address with Copy Address, Share Report… (the iOS share sheet, so you choose where the report goes) and Block; nothing is put on your clipboard unless you tap Copy Address. Settings → Contact & Legal → Email Support opens your email app. Nothing is sent unless you send it. A report on a person contains the name ATM Chat shows for them and their device ID. A reported message adds its sender's name as ATM Chat shows it and their device ID (in "Everyone Nearby", also the name the post carried, when it differs from the name that person chose), the message's type, its text (if it has any) and when it was sent. A report on the room as a whole names nobody. Media is never attached. If you gave someone a name in ATM Chat (Section 3.1), a report about them, its subject line included, shows that name rather than the one they chose. After Mail reports the email sent, the App offers to block the reported person, and says when messages waiting to send to them won't be sent. We receive whatever you send, along with your email address, and use it only to reply to you, to review and act on reports, and to cooperate with lawful requests from authorities (Section 16).
  • Alerts stay on your device — Alerts are created and shown by iOS on your device; nothing about them leaves it. Since version 1.2, message alerts show the message's text only if you turn on Settings → Show Message Previews (off by default), never for a chat with Auto-Delete on or for an "Everyone Nearby" post the App's filter hides, and the Lock Screen follows the Show Previews setting in iOS Settings.
  • No cloud sync — The App has no cloud sync, no server-side message storage, and no synchronization service of any kind, and it never sends your messages or other App data to us. Text you translate with iOS's Translate may also be kept by Apple, unless both languages were downloaded, under Apple's Translation & Privacy notice (see "Select Text, Translate and Look Up", above). Your messages are stored on your device and on the devices of the people you exchange them with (for "Everyone Nearby" posts, every device that receives them).   • No location data — The App does not request or use your geographic location. Videos and files you send keep any location metadata they already contain (Section 4.6).

We do not sell, rent, or trade any personal information. Apart from emails people choose to send us, including reports, we never possess it: your data lives on your device and on the devices of the people you message, not with us. Text you choose to translate or look up with iOS's own tools also goes to Apple, under Apple's notices (see "Select Text, Translate and Look Up", above).

3. On-Device Storage

ATM Chat stores data locally on your device: your profile, settings and small lists in UserDefaults, your device ID normally in the Keychain (Section 3.1), and your chats, media and related files in the App's own folder (Section 3.2). This data stays within the App's private sandbox, is not accessible to other apps, and is never sent to us by the App. It leaves your device only when the App sends it to nearby devices, some of it automatically (Section 4); when a link preview loads (Section 2); when you use iOS's own Translate or Look Up on text in the App, which may send that text to Apple (Section 2); when you choose to share, save, forward or report something yourself; and as part of your own device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) if you use them.

3.1 UserDefaults & Keychain

A small amount of data is stored using Apple's UserDefaults and Keychain systems. What the App sends (your own name, photo and device ID; the names and device IDs of the devices you're connected to at that moment, in neighbour announcements; and the delete requests, reaction changes and Read receipts waiting for the person they're meant for) is described in Section 4.2; the rest never leaves your device, except in a report you choose to send (Section 2) and in your own device backups:

  • Your display name and, if you set one, your profile photo (both shared with nearby devices as described in Section 4.2), and the network name other devices see during discovery, which is based on your display name and ends in a short random tag (Section 4.1). Since version 1.2, a name you set is at most 40 characters; the App still accepts longer names from devices on older versions (up to 256 characters).
  • A randomly generated unique identifier (UUID) that identifies your device to other ATM Chat devices — not derived from your Apple ID or other personal information. It is normally kept in the device Keychain, which is not restored to a different device. If the Keychain can't be used, it is kept in UserDefaults instead (as the earliest versions did), and that copy is included in device backups.
  • Display names of other ATM Chat users your device has learned, either by connecting to them or from the neighbour announcements of devices it connects to (Section 4.2), not only people you have chatted with. Since version 1.2, at most 2,000 are kept.
  • Names you give people (since version 1.2) — a name of your own for someone (Chat Info → Edit Name), shown on this device in place of the name they chose: on Home, in the chat, in Chat Info and in message alerts. Up to 40 characters, for up to 2,000 people. It is never sent to that person or to any nearby device, and they aren't told; it leaves your device only in a report you choose to send, which names them by it (Section 2). It is removed when you empty it in Edit Name or use Erase All Chats & Media; Clear Chat, Delete Chat and Block keep it. If you block someone you've named, from their chat or from Home, the list of people you've blocked keeps that name with their device ID until you unblock them, also through Erase All.
  • Previous names (since version 1.2) — when someone renames themselves, the name they had, with the date, so their Home row can say "was …" and Chat Info "Previously …": shown for 7 days, or until you open their Chat Info, and not while you've given them a name yourself; at most 500. A name past its 7 days is no longer shown or used, but stays stored until the list next changes (someone renames themselves, you open a Chat Info that shows one, or you name someone who had one), when it is removed. Erase All removes them all.
  • Per-conversation unread counts, and the chats you mark as unread. Since version 1.2, when you leave a chat, or the App goes to the background, with messages you haven't scrolled to, that number is kept so the chat list still shows them as unread.
  • The people you've blocked: each with a device ID and a name (the name shown when you blocked them, which can be a name you gave them). Since version 1.2, also a record of each block: when it started and ended, and the IDs and times of messages dropped while it lasted (up to 1,000 per person), kept until 7 days after you unblock them. All of this stays on your device and is never shared with anyone, including the people you've blocked.
  • Your notification preferences (which alert types you receive), per-chat mute list, and each chat's Auto-Delete setting (called Disappearing Messages in version 1.1 and earlier)
  • Since version 1.2, your other settings: Load Link Previews, Send Read Receipts, Send Typing Indicator, Show Message Previews, Sound for Everyone Nearby, Someone You Know Is Nearby (called peer-joined alerts before), Show Everyone Nearby, Keep Screen On and the voice-message playback speed; which version of the "Everyone Nearby" rules you agreed to; how far you got through first-run setup; and whether you tapped Got It on Home's "Ready for no signal?" card
  • Since version 1.2, for the App Store rating prompt only, to limit how often it can appear: a count of message deliveries, the date of the first one and the last three days with one, and the date and app version of the last prompt
  • Message requests (since version 1.2) — the IDs of chats you've accepted or written in, and of chats you've received a message in (so that a request's first message is recognised even when iOS starts the App while your phone is locked). On the first launch of 1.2, every chat you already had is added. Someone is added when you write to them, accept their request (on Home, or in the bar at the top of their chat), reply from an alert, choose Accept on their row under New People Nearby, or Accept in their empty chat; opening a chat doesn't add anyone. Deleting a chat with no message of yours left in it removes that person; deleting one that still has one keeps them accepted. Erase All empties both lists. The other person is never told.
  • Removed-message IDs (since version 1.2) — the IDs of messages removed from this device (by any delete, Clear Chat, Delete Chat, Erase All or Auto-Delete), with the time, for a week and up to 1,000 per chat, so that a re-sent or relayed copy can't bring a removed message back. No content.
  • Pending deletes (since version 1.2) — the IDs of your messages you deleted for everyone, kept until your next new direct connection with that person, then sent again on each new direct connection for 7 days (up to 500 per person). If you never connect directly again, they stay stored until you delete the App.
  • Pending reaction changes (since version 1.2) — every change you make to a reaction, per person (up to 200 messages per person), kept until it has been sent and then sent again on each new connection for a day after it first went.
  • Read receipts waiting for their sender (since version 1.2) — the IDs of messages you read while their sender was out of range, so the Read receipt can go when they're reachable again; only while Send Read Receipts is on, at most 200 per person and 20 people (the longest-waiting dropped first). They're sent, and cleared, when that person is next reachable, and only for messages still in your history; never to someone you've blocked or a message request, for whom they are kept meanwhile. Deleting the chat doesn't remove them (they then match no message, so nothing is sent); Erase All does. Message IDs only, no content.
  • Clock-lead floors (since version 1.2) — for up to 256 people, a lower bound for how far each one's clock runs ahead of or behind yours, learnt only from their direct messages that arrive within 15 seconds of their timestamp and used capped at 15 seconds, so that a later block can tell when a message was sent. Kept across launches; Delete Chat and Erase All don't remove it.
  • Network-name hints (since version 1.2) — up to 256 network names of devices this one has connected to, with their device IDs.
  • Saved to Photos (since version 1.2) — for each chat, the IDs of messages whose photo or video you saved to Photos, so that Save All Photos & Videos skips them; removed when you delete the chat.
  • "Everyone Nearby" reach counts (since version 1.2) — for each of your newest 200 room posts, how many devices it went to (shown as "Sent to N devices nearby"). Not part of the post, and not removed when you delete it.
  • When people were last nearby (since version 1.2) — for each person you have a chat with (not people you've blocked), the last time they were reachable, for at most 500 people, so the chat and Chat Info can say "Last nearby …". Recorded while the App is open, and, at most once a minute per person, whenever a contact's device identifies itself over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi or sends you a direct message, also in the background. Removed when you delete the chat, block them or use Erase All.
  • Someone You Know Is Nearby, last alerts (since version 1.2) — each person's device ID with the time of their last such alert, at most 200, so that a relaunch doesn't alert again within 30 minutes. Entries older than 30 minutes are no longer used, and are removed from storage the next time such an alert is shown; until then they stay, also while the switch is off. Erase All doesn't remove them.

This data is declared in our Privacy Manifest (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) under API category NSPrivacyAccessedAPICategoryUserDefaults with reason code CA92.1 (access to store and retrieve user preferences), as required by Apple.

3.2 ChatStorage (Custom Local Storage)

ATM Chat uses a custom on-device storage system called ChatStorage, plus a few related files in the App's own folder, to persist your messaging data locally. This includes:

  • Message history for each conversation, including the "Everyone Nearby" room: message text, sender name and device ID, time, replies, reactions (with the device ID of each person who reacted), media details such as file names, and, for a message sent or received while Auto-Delete is on, when it will be deleted on this device. Each conversation keeps up to 10,000 messages; older ones are removed. Since version 1.2, only a message deleted for everyone leaves a "This message was deleted" placeholder (sender, time and type, but no content); other deletions leave none.
  • Media files: copies of the photos, videos, voice messages, contact cards and files you send and receive. Since version 1.2, each contact card's file also records the card's name, so search can find it; it goes with the file.
  • Drafts (since version 1.2): what you were writing in each chat (the text and the message you were replying to), and a voice message cut short by leaving the chat, kept until you send or delete them, delete the chat, block that person from their chat or from a request, or use Erase All. Clear Chat doesn't remove a draft.
  • Conversation list (the list of people you have communicated with)
  • Delivery status of each message you send (sent, delivered or read). The App does not record when a message was read.
  • Offline queue: since version 1.2, everything you send in a one-to-one chat (texts, links, photos, videos, voice messages, contacts, files and forwards) waits in the queue until the other person's device confirms it, or for an attachment its file. Videos, and any attachment over 700 KB, wait for a direct Wi-Fi connection. Up to 500 wait per person (beyond that, the oldest is dropped), and a queued message more than 72 hours old is discarded instead of sent, whether or not the person comes back. A queued message whose Auto-Delete time runs out first is removed and never sent. In version 1.1 and earlier only text messages and links were queued.
  • Profile photos that other users' devices send when they connect to yours. Since version 1.2 they are re-encoded at a small size and at most 500 are kept.

All of this data resides within the App's iOS sandbox on your device. The App never uploads or syncs it, and never sends it to us or to any server. Like any app's data, it is included in your own device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) if you use them, and is restored with them. Apart from those backups, anything you choose to share, save, forward or report yourself, and text you choose to translate or look up with iOS's own tools (which iOS may send to Apple, Section 2), message content leaves your device only when it is sent to someone over the local network, directly or through other ATM Chat devices (see Section 4), and, when a link preview loads, when the link itself is requested from the website it points to (Section 2). Since version 1.2, if the history or offline-queue file is damaged and can't be decoded, it is kept in the same folder under a new name rather than overwritten.

How to delete (since version 1.2; version 1.1 and earlier offer only Delete for Everyone and Disappearing Messages):

  • Delete… on a message (long-press it, or in the full-screen viewer), after a confirmation: Delete on This iPhone (or iPad) for any message, including messages you received, "deleted" placeholders and "Everyone Nearby" posts; Delete for Everyone for your own messages in a one-to-one chat. Delete for Everyone removes your copy at once and sends a request to delete theirs, again on your next direct Wi-Fi or Bluetooth connection, whenever that is, and on each new one for 7 days after it; until then, or without a direct connection, their copy may remain, and a recipient using version 1.2 or later ignores delete requests relayed through other devices. You can't delete your posts from other people's devices.
  • Clear Chat (a chat's options menu, Chat Info, or a swipe or long-press on its Home row) removes a chat's messages and anything waiting to send; the chat stays listed and the person stays accepted.
  • Delete Chat (a swipe or long-press on Home) removes the chat and its messages, with what's waiting to send, its Auto-Delete setting, draft and mute, its Saved-to-Photos record and when that person was last nearby. If no message of yours is left in that chat (Clear Chat and Auto-Delete remove them), the person also becomes a message request again; if one is, they stay accepted. The confirmation says which.
  • Erase All Chats & Media (Settings, after a confirmation) deletes from this device every chat and every "Everyone Nearby" post, with their photos, videos and files (and any other file in the App's media folder), messages waiting to send, drafts and kept voice messages, Auto-Delete settings, mutes, pending reaction changes, unread counts and marks, who you've accepted or heard from, when people were last nearby, Read receipts waiting to be sent, the names you gave people and their previous names, which photos Save All saved, and the App's alerts in Notification Center. It keeps your name, photo and device ID, your settings, the people you've blocked (each with the name they were blocked under, which can be a name you gave them) and their block records, and the names and profile photos other people chose for themselves. It also keeps the lists in Section 3.1 that hold no message content, which are capped in size or expire (Section 10): removed-message IDs (which now include the erased messages, for a week), pending deletes, clock-lead floors, "Everyone Nearby" reach counts, network-name hints and the times of recent Someone You Know Is Nearby alerts. Nor does it remove a damaged history or offline-queue file set aside on this device (see above and Section 10), if there is one; that file can hold messages and stays until the App is deleted. Photos and videos you saved to Photos stay there, other people keep their copies, and copies in backups you already made stay there until those backups are replaced.
  • Auto-Delete (a one-to-one chat's options menu or Chat Info: 30 seconds, 5 minutes, 1 hour or 24 hours) applies only to messages sent or received after you set it: each is removed from your device when its time is up, with no placeholder, while the App is open. If the App is in the background or closed, or your phone is locked, iOS may not let it run, so the message and its files can stay on your device, and in any backup made meanwhile, until you next open the App, when they are removed at once. It is not removed from the other person's device, and they aren't told. Your queued messages whose time runs out are removed before anything is sent. Turning it off asks whether to keep messages already set to disappear. Messages from before you set it, including all history from version 1.1, are never touched by it.
  • Turning off Settings → Privacy → Show Everyone Nearby deletes the room's posts from this device, after asking when there are any; while it's off, new posts aren't stored or alerted (your device still passes them on, Section 4.2).
  • A photo, video or other file is deleted with its message when no other message uses it.

To remove all of the App's stored data, delete ATM Chat from your device — iOS removes everything in the App's sandbox (message history, media, conversation list, and preferences) on uninstall. Two narrow exceptions, both standard iOS behavior: the random device UUID stored in the iOS Keychain (Section 3.1) may persist on the device after the App is deleted until it is removed, and device backups you made before deletion may retain copies of previously stored data. Neither of these ever reaches us.

4. Local Networking & Mesh Messaging

Local networking is the primary feature of ATM Chat. The App lets nearby devices message each other directly, or through other nearby ATM Chat devices, using two Apple frameworks: MultipeerConnectivity (Wi-Fi) and CoreBluetooth (Bluetooth Low Energy). This section provides comprehensive detail about how your data is handled during messaging sessions.

4.1 How It Works

  • Messaging is fully peer-to-peer — no internet connection is used or required to send or receive messages. All messaging travels over local Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), directly between devices or relayed by other nearby ATM Chat devices (Section 4.3). Messaging passes no message data through our servers, Apple's servers, or any internet service. (iOS's Translate and Look Up, which you can use on a message's text, are separate: iOS may send that text to Apple; see Section 2.) The App's only internet access is the link-preview fetch described in Section 2, which is automatic in version 1.1 and earlier and, since version 1.2, off unless you turn it on.
  • Radios, not a network — The App needs Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on, not a joined Wi-Fi network or the internet, so it also works in Airplane Mode once you turn both back on in Control Center. With Bluetooth alone, texts and small files go, but videos and any attachment over 700 KB wait for a direct Wi-Fi connection.
  • The App uses Apple's Bonjour protocol (via MultipeerConnectivity) and BLE advertising (via CoreBluetooth) to discover nearby devices that are running ATM Chat. While the App is open, your device advertises itself, and over Bluetooth it keeps doing so while the App is in the background. The MultipeerConnectivity advertisement carries your network name, so nearby devices looking for ATM Chat can see it before any connection. The network name is your display name with spaces and punctuation turned into hyphens (a name with no letters or digits becomes "ATMChat-User"), followed by a short random tag, for example "Ali-7F3A". The tag isn't your device ID, but it stays the same when you change your name, and Erase All doesn't change it, so someone nearby could recognise your device after a rename; deleting the App removes it. Since version 1.2 the advertisement also carries a version number and a flag saying the device accepts re-link invitations, and no longer names the App. Since version 1.2 the App's own Bluetooth advertisement carries no name, only the App's service ID (in earlier versions it carried the name "ATMChat"), and never your device ID (Section 4.2 describes what a device that connects can obtain). From those service IDs, nearby devices can still tell that ATM Chat is running; first-run setup says so before you choose a name. Your device connects automatically to the ATM Chat devices it finds. Since version 1.2, while the App is in the background it declines new Wi-Fi invitations, and nearby devices link again once it's back on screen.
  • Once devices discover each other, a direct connection is established. All subsequent data exchange occurs over this local peer-to-peer link.
  • No message logs exist on any server. Because messaging never touches the internet and we operate no backend infrastructure, there is no server that could store, log, intercept, or retain any of your messages. We have no ability to read, access, or recover your messages. The exception is text you choose to translate or look up with iOS's own tools: iOS may send it to Apple, under Apple's own privacy notices (Section 2).

4.2 Data Transmitted During Sessions

During an active local messaging session, the following types of data may be transmitted between connected devices:

  • Text messages — The content of messages you type and send to other people.
  • Photos — Images you select from your photo library, take with your camera, paste (Paste Photo, which reads the copied photo only when you choose it, after iOS asks you to allow pasting) or drag onto a chat, to send to other people.
  • Videos — Video files you select from your photo library or drag onto a chat.
  • Voice messages — Audio recordings you create within the App.
  • Contact cards — Contact information you explicitly choose to share, including the contact's photo, re-encoded as a 256-pixel square, when it has one. Since version 1.2, the send preview lists every detail the card carries, and you can leave out any detail, or the photo, with a tap: the card is rebuilt on your device with only the ticked details, and the name always goes.
  • Files — Documents and files you explicitly select, or drag onto a chat, to send.
  • Captions — A caption typed in the send preview is sent as a separate text message right after the photo, video, file or contact.
  • Replies — A reply carries a short quote of the message it answers. Since version 1.2, a file, a contact or the first of several photos sent as a reply carries the quote too, as a single photo already did.
  • Forwards — Since version 1.2, Forward can send a message to several people at once; each gets their own copy.
  • Delivery receipts — Confirmation signals indicating that a message has been delivered to the recipient's device. Since version 1.2, a device also confirms when a photo, video, voice message, contact or file has arrived, and the sender keeps the item queued until then. A receipt carries only message IDs; one with no direct route to its sender is passed to neighbouring ATM Chat devices to carry on, never to someone you've blocked.
  • Read receipts — Signals indicating that a recipient has opened and viewed your message. Since version 1.2, they're not sent if you turn off Settings → Privacy → Send Read Receipts (Delivered still goes), and never to a message request until you reply or accept (then the held ones go); Read goes only for messages that have been on screen, and for messages you read while their sender was out of range it goes when they're reachable again (Section 3.1). A receipt lists only newly read messages (at most 200), plus the newest 50 again after a connection change.
  • Message reactions — Emoji reactions you attach to specific messages. Since version 1.2, every change you make to a reaction is sent at once when possible and again on each new connection for a day after it first went; one made while the person can't get it waits until they can (Section 3.1).
  • Delete requests — When you use Delete for Everyone on a message you sent in a one-to-one chat, a request to remove it is sent to the person you sent it to, if they are reachable, and since version 1.2 again on your next direct connection, whenever that is, and on each new one for 7 days after it. A recipient using version 1.2 or later removes a message only at the request of the person who wrote it, and only when the request arrives over a direct connection; requests relayed through other devices are ignored, so their copy may remain (Section 3.2). Delete for Everyone isn't available in the "Everyone Nearby" room.
  • Display name, profile photo and device UUID — These are sent automatically, as soon as a connection is made, to every ATM Chat device yours connects to (not only people you chat with, but also, for example, someone listed under New People Nearby), so devices can identify each other. Your display name and UUID are also part of every message you send, including messages relayed through other devices. Over Bluetooth, your display name and UUID are sent to any device that connects to the App's Bluetooth messaging service and subscribes to it, without pairing, so a nearby Bluetooth device that isn't running ATM Chat can obtain them too (but not your photo). In version 1.1 and earlier, any device that connected to either of the App's two Bluetooth services could also read your UUID directly from it. The UUID is randomly generated and not derived from your Apple ID, phone number or other personal information, but it is sent together with your display name, does not change when you change your name, and can survive reinstalling the App (Section 3.2). Since version 1.2 you can remove your photo (Settings → Remove Photo); people on 1.2 then see your initial, while older versions keep showing the last photo they received.

4.3 Mesh Routing & Privacy Implications

ATM Chat can pass messages through other nearby ATM Chat devices, so two people who aren't in range of each other can still reach each other through a device in between. You can send a one-to-one message only to someone ATM Chat can reach directly or through one other ATM Chat device (someone connected to a device you're connected to), and it normally goes through just that one device. If that device can't deliver it, for example because a connection has just dropped, the message can be handed to other connected devices, and they can do the same, through up to four devices in a row. So a message can occasionally be carried by more devices than the one in between, and each of them receives it unencrypted (Section 4.4). "Everyone Nearby" posts are passed on through up to four devices in a row as a matter of course. When your device chooses the route, it avoids devices of people you've blocked if another device can carry the message; other devices don't know who you've blocked, and your device can't control where they pass it next.

Important privacy details about relaying:

  • Intermediate devices act as relays only. When a message passes through an intermediate device, the ATM Chat app on that device forwards it onward automatically, including while the App is in the background. Relayed messages addressed to someone else are never displayed to the relay device's user and are never saved into that device's chat history. Posts in the "Everyone Nearby" room are different: every device they reach, relays included, shows and stores them (Section 4.2).
  • No persistent relay logs. Relaying happens in memory; intermediate devices save no copy of the messages they forward. To avoid forwarding the same message twice, a relay remembers each packet's random ID (not its content) in memory only, for at most the last 5,000 packets. A relay can also see which device UUIDs a packet is from and to.
  • Honest limitation: relayed traffic is not end-to-end encrypted (see Section 4.4). While the ATM Chat app never shows relayed content, a technically sophisticated user with modified software on the relay path could in principle inspect relayed data. Treat these conversations like conversations in a shared physical space.

4.4 Transport Security

Local peer-to-peer sessions currently run without transport-layer encryption. This is a deliberate engineering trade-off: encrypted MultipeerConnectivity sessions proved significantly slower and less reliable over mixed Wi-Fi + Bluetooth links between several devices, and dependable delivery is the App's core purpose. What this means in practice:

  • ATM Chat never sends your messages over the internet, so they cannot be intercepted remotely — an eavesdropper would have to be physically nearby, within local Wi-Fi/Bluetooth range, while you chat. (When a link preview loads, the shared link itself is requested from the website it points to; see Section 2. iOS's Translate and Look Up are Apple features and may send the text you choose to Apple; see Section 2.)
  • Within that local radio range, transmitted data is not encrypted. Treat ATM Chat conversations like conversations in a shared physical space.
  • We recommend not sending highly sensitive information (passwords, ID numbers, financial details) over any local network between devices, including ATM Chat.
  • A future update may add transport encryption; if the trade-offs change, this policy will be updated accordingly (see Section 31).

4.5 Session Lifecycle

When a messaging session ends (by disconnecting, moving out of range, or quitting the App from the app switcher), the peer-to-peer connection is terminated. While the App is in the background or the phone is locked, iOS pauses its Wi-Fi (MultipeerConnectivity) connections, so it keeps working only over Bluetooth links that are already connected: over those it can receive messages, send what you reply from an alert, and relay other people's messages, and iOS can relaunch it in the background for Bluetooth events (since version 1.2, with networking started even without a screen, though not before the phone's first unlock after it starts). Since version 1.2, anything you send to someone with no Bluetooth link to them waits on your device and goes once the App is open again. When the App returns to the screen, it finds dead links and invites nearby devices again; invitations go only while it is open on screen. Settings → Keep Screen On (off by default) stops the phone locking by itself while the App is in front. Message history that has been saved to ChatStorage (Section 3.2) persists locally on your device. No session data is transmitted to any server, and no session metadata is retained beyond what is stored locally on your own device. Since version 1.2, "Someone You Know Is Nearby" alerts are shown only for people you've chatted with, only while the App is in the background, when they come into range, and at most once per person every 30 minutes (Section 3.1).

4.6 Media & File Privacy

Photos, videos, voice messages, contact cards, and files you send through ATM Chat are transmitted to the recipient's device over the local peer-to-peer connection. They are never uploaded to any server, cloud storage service, or content delivery network. Videos, and any other attachment over 700 KB, are sent only over a direct Wi-Fi connection; since version 1.2 they wait on your device until there is one (Section 3.2). Other media up to 700 KB sent to someone who isn't directly connected passes through relaying devices on the way, in memory only (Section 4.3). Once transmitted, the media is stored on the sender's and recipient's devices (and in their device backups, if they use them); what the recipient does with it after that, such as saving, sharing or forwarding it, is up to them.

Photos you send from your library or take with the camera, and photos you paste or drag in, are downscaled and re-encoded on your device before sending, which also strips embedded metadata such as location (EXIF). Videos and files are sent as they are, without re-encoding, so they keep any metadata they contain (a video can include where it was recorded), and a file is sent with its original file name. A contact card is rebuilt on your device with only the details you leave ticked (Section 4.2). A reply to a photo or video shows a small thumbnail that each device makes from the copy it already has; nothing more is sent for it. If you choose to save received photos or videos to your own photo library, the App uses Apple's add-only photo access — it can add the items you explicitly save but can never read, browse, or access anything else in your library. Photos, videos, voice messages, contacts and files offer neither Select Text nor Translate; for the text of a text or link message, see Section 2.

17. Message Delivery Disclaimer

ATM Chat is an offline, peer-to-peer messaging application for people nearby. Message delivery is best-effort only and is not guaranteed. Due to the inherent nature of local networking between devices:

  • Range limitations — Messages can only be delivered to devices within range of your local Wi-Fi or Bluetooth Low Energy signal, either directly or through other ATM Chat devices in between (Section 4.3).
  • Relay availability — If there is no connected path of ATM Chat devices between you and the intended recipient, messages cannot be delivered. You can message someone only if ATM Chat can reach them directly or through one device in between; a device in between that loses its link may pass the message further, up to four devices in a row (Section 4.3). An "Everyone Nearby" post passes through at most four devices in a row. Over Bluetooth or through another device, files larger than 700 KB cannot be sent, and videos can only be sent over a direct Wi-Fi connection.
  • Session dependency — Messages can only travel while the App is running on the sending and receiving devices (and on any device relaying them) and those devices are connected, directly or through another device. While the App is in the background or the phone is locked, iOS pauses its Wi-Fi connections, so messages travel only over a Bluetooth link that's already connected; iOS decides when the App runs, and a device that is switched off, out of range, or on which the App has been force-quit receives nothing. To receive reliably, keep the App open (Settings → Keep Screen On can help). There is no server to queue or store messages for later delivery.
  • Environmental factors — Physical obstacles, interference, device settings, and distance may affect connection quality and message delivery reliability.
  • Local queuing only — Since version 1.2, everything you send in a one-to-one chat (texts, links, photos, videos, voice messages, contacts, files and forwards) waits in an on-device queue when the recipient can't get it, and the App sends it again until their device confirms it arrived (for an attachment, its file). It goes when you're both nearby with the App open, directly or through one device in between; in the background, only over a Bluetooth link that's already connected, and otherwise it waits until the App is open again. Videos and any attachment over 700 KB wait for a direct Wi-Fi connection. Queued messages older than 72 hours are discarded unsent, and at most 500 wait per person (the oldest are dropped first). A queued message that you delete, or whose Auto-Delete time runs out, is removed from the queue and not sent. In version 1.1 and earlier only texts and links are queued, and a queued message deleted for everyone or expired is still delivered when the person reconnects. Posts in the "Everyone Nearby" room are never queued: they reach only the devices linked, directly or through others, when you post, nothing re-shares them with people who arrive later, and a post that reached nobody shows Not Delivered with Try Again. There is no internet or server path for delivery, so a message to someone who never comes back into range will never be delivered.
  • Best-effort delivery — Messages may be lost, delayed, duplicated, or fail to arrive without notice to you. If you share session access — for example by posting in the "Everyone Nearby" room or by chatting in a crowded environment — messages may also be received by unintended people nearby.
  • Delete for Everyone is limited — It is available only for messages you sent in a one-to-one chat (not in the "Everyone Nearby" room), and asks first. It removes the other person's copy only if their App receives the delete request. Nothing is sent to someone who can't be reached when you delete; since version 1.2 the request is sent on your next direct connection, whenever that is, and on each new one for 7 days after it. If none of those arrives, or you never connect directly again, their copy stays. If the other person uses version 1.2 or later, their App ignores deletes relayed through other devices, so their copy is removed only if your devices are directly connected; since version 1.2, the App tells you when you are not directly connected. Earlier versions also accept relayed deletes. Copies the recipient has already saved to Photos, forwarded, shared or captured in a screenshot are not affected.
